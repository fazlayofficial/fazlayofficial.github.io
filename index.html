Aspiring Ph.D. Candidate. Researcher in Edge AI, TinyML & Healthcare Microsystems.

Welcome! I am Fazlay Rabby, working on Embedded Machine Learning (TinyML), Edge AI, and their applications in robotics and healthcare systems.

My research focuses on deploying deep learning models on resource-constrained embedded systems—enabling intelligent systems to operate on microcontrollers without cloud connectivity. I work on model compression, hardware-aware neural architectures, and real-time biosignal processing for assistive robotics, wearable health monitors, and industrial automation.

I am fortunate to work with Dr. A.B.M. Sayeed Ud Doulah at Southeast University for over a year on healthcare and machine learning projects. We are currently working on “Quantifying Sustained Attention and Distraction in ADHD Children Using Augmented Reality Literacy Tasks and Automated Computer Vision” under his supervision. Previously, I worked as an Application Engineer at ANTT Robotics Ltd, leading firmware development for industrial IoT and EduTech systems. I received my Bachelor of Science in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the University of Liberal Arts Bangladesh (ULAB) in 2024, completing my thesis under Dr. Sayeed Ud Doulah’s supervision.

I am actively seeking Ph.D. opportunities for Fall 2026.

Research Vision

My long-term research vision is to democratize intelligent embedded systems by enabling sophisticated AI to run entirely on-device, without cloud dependency. I aim to develop TinyML frameworks that are adaptive, energy-efficient, and clinically trustworthy—bringing advanced healthcare monitoring and assistive robotics to resource-constrained environments where conventional AI systems cannot reach. These systems will empower underserved communities with life-saving technologies and enable autonomous systems to operate reliably in the physical world, regardless of infrastructure limitations.

  2. COMPAS
    TinySenseNet: A Lightweight sEMG-IMU Fusion Network Using TinyML for Mechanical Arm Control in Low-Resource Settings
    Fazlay Rabby, Md. Rifat Aknda, Mumtahina Tasnim Mahi, and 2 more authors
    In 2025 IEEE 2nd International Conference on Computing, Applications and Systems (COMPAS), 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This paper introduces TinySenseNet, a lightweight and memory-efficient deep learning model for real-time robotic arm control using multimodal surface electromyography (sEMG) and inertial measurement unit (IMU) signals. Optimized for embedded deployment, the system processes 150 ms input windows to achieve low-latency gesture recognition with TinyML. The architecture employs depthwise 1D convolutions and an attention mechanism for efficient temporal feature extraction and robust multimodal fusion. Unlike CNNs, LSTMs, and SVMs — which are either computationally heavy, slow, or noise-sensitive — TinySenseNet offers both efficiency and resilience on low-power hardware. Key innovations include cross-modal delay features that capture timing between muscle activation and motion onset, a confidence-based actuation mechanism with haptic feedback to improve operational safety, and a culturally adapted gesture set for enhanced usability. Evaluation across controlled lab settings, real-world noisy conditions, and clinical trials with transradial amputees at Dhaka Medical College demonstrates strong generalization. TinySenseNet achieved 97.2% accuracy for able-bodied users, 95.7% for amputees, and 93.3% in real-world scenarios, outperforming CNN, LSTM, and SVM baselines. With a model size of 1.46 KB and 23.8 ms end-to-end latency, TinySenseNet provides an affordable, scalable, and clinically viable solution for assistive robotic control in low-resource environments.

  3. ICEEIE
    Scalable Hand Gesture Recognition from Surface Electromyography (sEMG) Signals Using a Hybrid Deep Learning Model Evaluated on Diverse Datasets
    Fazlay Rabby, Rajdeep Das, Md. Musfiqur Rahman, and 2 more authors
    In 2025 9th International Conference On Electrical, Electronics And Information Engineering (ICEEIE), 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Surface electromyography (sEMG) provides a non-invasive and effective way for hand gestures recognition. It plays a crucial role in areas such as prosthetics, human-computer interaction (HCI), and rehabilitation systems. In this study, a hybrid deep learning approach was proposed that combines multi-scale one-dimensional convolutional neural networks (1D CNNs), bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) layers and an attention mechanism. The hybrid approach is a lightweight and scalable system. It performs efficiently on time-series classification of sEMG signals. The study also focuses on collecting a novel dataset which was recorded from 26 individuals of different age and gender group. The basic five hand gestures samples were collected by using a user-friendly and customized application. The proposed model achieves an impressive test accuracy of 97% while maintaining a compact of 616 K parameters. This lightweight model becomes more suitable for real-time deployment on low-power edge devices. The study further explored cross-data evaluation using the publicly available Ninapro dataset DB1 for model’s robustness and generalizability. By using a hybrid feature extraction technique with deep neural modeling, the proposed method improves both interpretability and performance which offers a practical solution for real-time applications. This study contributes a scalable data collection framework, a novel benchmark dataset and a hybrid model advancing in sEMGbased gesture recognition.
